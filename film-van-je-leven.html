The challenge

For my graduation project with the City of Amsterdam, I explored how 16–23-year-olds could feel more in control of the time they spend on social media. I wanted to understand what happens between picking up your phone for a reason and realising much later that you’re still scrolling.

What I learned

I combined a diary study, a survey and six interviews with conversations with experts. The research pointed to a familiar pattern: scrolling can start automatically, and people often only notice how much time has passed after they stop. That gave me a design question: where could a small prompt help someone pause and decide what they want to do next?

What I designed

I built the app around a question: whose life is filling your film roll? The introduction sets up that idea. An intention check gives users a chance to decide why they’re opening social media. A film roll and weekly recap give them a place to collect and revisit moments from their own lives.

What testing changed

The opening connected with participants, but six scenes were too many. The screen-time message also felt a little harsh, and the difference between recording your own moments and watching someone else’s life needed to come through sooner. For the next iteration, I’d shorten the opening and test a gentler prompt.
